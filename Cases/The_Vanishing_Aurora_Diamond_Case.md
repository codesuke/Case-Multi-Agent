# CASE BOOK

# The Vanishing Aurora Diamond

AI Mystery Detective Team Workshop

At 8:20 PM, Northbridge Museum lost power. Four minutes later, the priceless Aurora Diamond was gone from a locked display case. The glass was unbroken. Four people had possible motives. One access record changed the investigation.

All people, places, and events in this case are fictional. This is a participant edition. It contains no sealed facilitator solution.

## Investigation Briefing

Determine the most likely explanation for the disappearance of the Aurora Diamond. A good investigation does not simply name a suspect. It connects claims to evidence, separates facts from inference, considers alternatives, and states uncertainty.

### Mission

- Reconstruct the timeline.
- Evaluate every clue.
- Compare all suspects using the same criteria.
- Challenge the leading theory.
- Recommend the most likely solution and explain its uncertainty.

### Rules of Investigation

- Use only information contained in this case book.
- Do not invent evidence, witnesses, or events.
- A motive is not proof.
- Access by a card does not automatically prove who held it.
- Identify contradictions and innocent explanations.
- Every final claim must cite evidence IDs, and the verdict requires human review.

## Case File 001

### Incident Report

At 8:00 PM, curator Dr. Mira Sen displayed the Aurora Diamond inside a locked glass case in the Grand Gallery of Northbridge Museum. At 8:20 PM, a power failure darkened the gallery for four minutes. Emergency lighting was limited. At 8:30 PM, staff discovered that the diamond was missing. The display glass remained intact and showed no sign of forced entry.

## Witness Statements

### Dr. Mira Sen, Curator

“I inspected the diamond and locked the case at 8:00 PM. The electronic system accepts authorized cards and continues recording during a power failure because it has a battery. I did not reopen the case.”

### Lena Ortiz, Facilities Coordinator

“The generator alarm appeared shortly before the blackout. I went to the basement and worked there from approximately 8:19 until 8:26. I crossed the wet courtyard earlier while inspecting an exterior door.”

### Theo Park, Event Host

“I remained on the stage during the programme. The audience and camera were facing me. I did not enter the Grand Gallery during the blackout.”

### Arjun Vale, Archivist

“I worked in the archive throughout the blackout. My access card remained in the jacket I had left inside the archive. I carried a catalogue folder when I left because I was returning documents.”

### Sofia Reed, Journalist

“I was interviewing visitors in the lobby when the lights failed. I continued recording comments on my phone. Several guests were standing with me.”

### Three Lobby Guests, Combined Note

“Each guest independently remembers speaking with Sofia during part or all of the blackout. None reports seeing her enter the gallery.”

Investigator note: A statement is evidence, but it may be incomplete or mistaken. Look for independent support.

## Investigation Challenge

Complete the original investigation first. Then run the case again without Evidence E, the blue velvet fibers. Predict whether the leading suspect remains the same and whether confidence should decrease.

### Optional Variations

- Strengthen Lena’s generator alibi with a timestamped maintenance log.
- Add a witness who saw someone carrying Arjun’s jacket.
- Require the Lead Detective to cite evidence IDs for every claim.
- Run the Timeline Reconciler, Evidence Collector, and Suspect Analyst independently before synthesis.
- Limit each report to 100 words and observe information loss.

### Best Next Evidence

- Recover and inspect the access card for handling evidence.
- Examine archive and corridor footage before and after the blackout.
- Search the folder and archive under appropriate fictional-case procedures.
- Compare fibers scientifically rather than relying only on color.
- Verify whether anyone could access Arjun’s jacket or borrow his card.
