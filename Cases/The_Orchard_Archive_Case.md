# CASE BOOK

# The Orchard Archive

AI Mystery Detective Team Workshop

During the harvest auction at fictional Bramblewood Orchard, the only signed copy of a century-old land covenant disappeared from the farm office. The document restricts commercial development on the northern field. This participant edition contains no facilitator solution.

## Investigation Briefing

Determine the most likely explanation for the missing covenant. Connect every claim to evidence, distinguish facts from interpretations, compare every suspect fairly, and describe the uncertainty that remains.

### Rules of Investigation

- Use only the information in this case book.
- Do not invent witnesses, documents, motives, or forensic results.
- A key or location record can be incomplete evidence.
- A motive is not proof that someone acted.
- Cite evidence IDs for every important factual claim.

## Case File 024

### Incident Report

At 3:30 PM, Bramblewood Orchard opened its harvest auction to local buyers. The farm office remained open for event administration, while a small wall safe held the original 1926 land covenant. At 4:05 PM, owner Ruth Calder checked that the signed original was in the safe before meeting a prospective buyer.

At 4:32 PM, Ruth returned to find the safe door closed but unlatched and the covenant missing. No other papers appeared disturbed. The office has a front door facing the auction yard and a rear door leading to a packing shed. The rear door's latch had been reported unreliable before the event.

### Central Questions

- When could the covenant have been taken?
- Who knew, or could reasonably learn, where it was stored?
- Was the safe opened by someone with the combination, by an already-open safe, or through another explanation?
- What does the rear-door evidence establish, if anything?
- Which next item of evidence would most reduce uncertainty?

## Witness Statements

### Ruth Calder, orchard owner

“At 4:05 I opened the safe to show the covenant to a buyer’s solicitor, then put it back and turned the dial. I met Elin and two buyers by the auction platform soon afterward. I only noticed the safe was not latched when I came back at 4:32.”

### Daniel Pike, orchard manager

“I was in the packing shed because rain was coming and the apple crates had to be moved under cover. I use my master key for the office when I need supplies, but I have never been told the safe combination. I saw the rear office door move in the wind earlier that day.”

### Elin Shaw, land agent

“I wanted a copy of the covenant so I could explain it to interested buyers, not hide it. From around 4:10 I was at the platform with bid sheets. Ruth came and went while talking to people. I did not enter the office after lunch.”

### Noor Patel, local historian

“I was photographing the old cider press and the tools displayed outside the front office. I saw Daniel carry crates in the packing shed, but I could not see the rear office door from where I stood. I do not know the safe combination.”

### Auction Clerk, Signed Note

“At 4:16 PM, I asked Ruth to settle a bidder-registration question. She spoke with me at the platform for about four minutes. Elin was sorting bid sheets at the same platform during that conversation.”

### Buyer Representative, Signed Note

“Between roughly 4:21 and 4:27 PM, Ruth and I discussed the northern field in the auction yard. I cannot account for her before or after that conversation. I noticed she had a folded paper in one hand, but I did not read it.”
