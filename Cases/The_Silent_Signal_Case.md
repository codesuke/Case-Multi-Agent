# CASE BOOK

# The Silent Signal

AI Mystery Detective Team Workshop

At 6:10 PM, a prototype emergency-radio transmitter disappeared from the
control room at Halcyon Community Radio. It had been present at the end of a
test broadcast. The control-room door was not forced, and its access log
contains one entry that conflicts with a staff member's account.

All people, places, organizations, and events in this case are fictional.
This is a participant edition. It contains no facilitator solution.

## Investigation Briefing

Determine the most likely explanation for the transmitter's disappearance.
Your investigation should connect each claim to evidence, distinguish facts
from interpretations, compare every suspect fairly, and state what remains
unknown. A plausible motive or an access record alone is not proof of who
acted.

### Mission

- Reconstruct the relevant timeline.
- Evaluate each item in the evidence register.
- Compare the suspects' means, opportunity, access, and alibi support.
- Challenge the leading explanation with credible alternatives.
- Propose the next evidence to collect before a human decision.

### Rules of Investigation

- Use only the information in this case book.
- Do not invent events, witnesses, or forensic results.
- Treat a keycard record as proof that a card was used, not proof of who held it.
- Identify innocent explanations for suspicious clues when they are possible.
- Keep every final claim traceable to one or more evidence IDs.

## Case File 017

### Incident Report

Halcyon Community Radio was preparing a public demonstration of a portable
emergency-radio transmitter. At 5:45 PM, technician Imani Brooks placed the
device in a foam-lined transport case in the locked control room after a
successful broadcast test. At 6:00 PM, the building's fire-alarm system began
a scheduled drill, requiring staff and visitors to assemble in the courtyard.

At 6:10 PM, Imani returned to the control room and found the transport case
open and empty. The control-room door and the device case showed no damage.
The transmitter is small enough to fit inside a messenger bag, but it cannot
transmit without a removable battery pack that was stored separately.

### Central Questions

- When was the transmitter most likely removed?
- How was the control room entered without damage?
- Who had the means and opportunity to take it?
- Does the access record identify a person or only a card?
- Which missing evidence would most change the leading explanation?

## Witness Statements

### Imani Brooks, technician

“I put the transmitter in its foam case at 5:45 and latched it. When the drill
started, I went straight outside. I did not return to the control room until
we were told the drill was over. I had loaned my keycard to Mara earlier so
she could collect a presentation adapter, but she returned it before the
alarm.”

### Mara Voss, station manager

“I was helping visitors reach the courtyard from about 5:59 until the
all-clear. I used Imani’s card around 5:50 because I had misplaced mine, then
gave it back to her at the reception desk. I did not enter the control room
during the drill.”

### Owen Price, volunteer engineer

“I left at about 5:52 to get shielded cable from the electronics shop. My
phone battery was low, so I paid cash and did not receive a digital receipt. I
never had permission to enter the control room.”

### Celia Ward, freelance reporter

“The alarm was an opportunity to get reactions from people outside, so I
recorded short interviews in the courtyard. I did not go inside after the
alarm began. I saw someone in a dark station jacket return through the side
entrance, but I could not see their face.”

### Reception volunteer, signed note

“At approximately 5:56 PM, I saw Mara hand Imani a staff card beside the
reception desk. I could not see the name or number on the card. Both women
then walked toward the courtyard when the alarm sounded.”

### Building safety officer, drill log

“The alarm began at 6:00 PM. The main courtyard camera recorded the assembly
area continuously from 6:01 until 6:08 PM. The west-side service corridor was
not covered by a camera during the drill because its camera was under repair.”
